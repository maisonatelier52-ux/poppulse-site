Pakistan’s finance minister presents the country’s next budget that hikes defense spending

People visit a store to buy room air cooler, in Lahore, Pakistan, Friday, June 12, 2026.

Prepared by PopPulse Editorial · Editorial desk
Published July 30, 2026Updated September 30, 20264 min read

At a glance

What you need to know

  • The draft budget raises defense spending by 18% for the fiscal year beginning July 1.
  • Development funding is reduced while salaried workers receive modest tax relief.
  • The plan must be read alongside slow growth, high living costs and regional-security pressures.

ISLAMABAD — Pakistan’s finance minister Friday unveiled the government’s 2019 budget to parliamentarians, a blueprint that boosts defense expenditure by 18 percent, cuts development programs and provides small tax relief to salaried people.

The idea highlights the challenges Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif’s government is facing in addressing sluggish development, soaring living costs and uncertainty, amid regional tensions and the war in the Middle East.

Pakistan has emerged as a major mediator to bring Iran and the United States to the negotiation table in a bid to resolve the current war. A fragile ceasefire is holding but the talks have stagnated.

Pakistan is also in dispute with neighboring Afghanistan, accusing Kabul of harboring Pakistani terrorists battling the government in Islamabad - an allegation Kabul denies. Hundreds have been slain since conflict flared up again in February.

Under the 2019 budget, total spending would rise marginally to 18.77 trillion rupees ($67.49 billion), Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb told Parliament.

Pakistan’s fiscal year 2026-27 begins July 1. The plan will be voted on by lawmakers later this month.

The government is targeting 4 percent growth and an inflation rate of 8.2 percent for next year, but price pressures remain a big worry for consumers already battered by years of rising costs, Aurangzeb said.

The budget closely mirrors provisions under Pakistan's ongoing $7 billion program with the International Monetary Fund that has pushed for increased revenues, wider taxation and structural reforms to cut persistent fiscal deficits.

Why this matters

Pakistan's fiscal choices affect public services now and growth capacity later. The budget also signals how regional conflict is reshaping domestic priorities.

What to consider next

Parliament can amend the draft. Watch the enacted totals, the financing assumptions and the first quarterly execution report rather than treating the proposal as final spending.
