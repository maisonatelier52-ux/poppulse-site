Amazon to boost spending on AI and other technology by $20 billion after strong Q2 results

Boxes sit on a conveyor belt at an Amazon storage warehouse in Tepotzotlan, Mexico, May 22, 2025.

Prepared by PopPulse Editorial · Editorial desk
Published June 22, 2026Updated September 30, 20264 min read

At a glance

What you need to know

  • Amazon lifted planned capital spending from $200 billion to $220 billion.
  • AWS sales rose 37% in the second quarter, up from 28% growth in the previous period.
  • The spending plan covers more than AI, including robots, semiconductors and satellites.

NEW YORK — Sales in the cloud computing unit named AWS grew 37 percent in the April-June period, quicker than the 28 percent rate in the previous quarter and marking the greatest rate of growth in 18 quarters, the Seattle-based company said Thursday.

We now expect capital expenditures to be $220 billion, and that includes spending on robots, semiconductors, and satellites, CEO and President Andy Jassy told investors on the call. That is up from a $200 billion investment plan unveiled in February and considerably ahead of $128 billion in total capital spending for all of last year. The rise was mostly due to increasing memory chip costs, Jassy said.Jassy warned investors that even at the $220 billion threshold, Amazon won’t have the capacity to service all of the demand it has this year.

“I think this dynamic will be in play in 2027 as well,” he continued. “We already have a staggering amount of demand for 2028.” Its shares jumped more than 9% in after-hours trade.

Investors have been monitoring Amazon’s quarterly earnings attentively for any signs that the company’s huge investments in AI are starting to pay off.

The Seattle-based corporation gave a conservative sales projection for the current quarter.

Amazon was one of the last of the internet giants to publish its quarterly profit results. The papers provide investors with a look at AI spending and cloud computing growth throughout the industry.

Last week, Alphabet, Google's parent company, posted better-than-expected second-quarter revenue, helped by an 82% rise in its cloud division. But the shares slid as the IT giant boosted its full-year capital expenditure target to between $195 billion and $205 billion. That's up from its earlier projection of $180 billion to $190 billion.

Microsoft, which reported a higher profit for the latest quarter than experts predicted, said its Azure cloud division posted significant growth. The corporation did not disclose a large rise in its ambitions for AI spending. That helped to raise the shares

In a statement Thursday, Jassy said AWS is “booming” and that its AI and chips businesses are each on track to surpass more than $25 billion in annual revenue. The company achieved record delivery speeds for its Prime members in the first half of the year, he said, with 40% more items delivered same-day or overnight in stores.

“There’s a lot to be excited about and we have a lot more coming for our customers in the second half of the year and beyond.” he said.

Amazon’s latest quarter results showed demand for Amazon’s services and technology keeps expanding.

Amazon has made large deals with Meta, Anthropic and Open AI in April.

Amazon announced in April what it called a “major expansion” of its cooperation with the maker of the popular chatbot, a day after the artificial intelligence company indicated it was reducing its links to longstanding sponsor Microsoft.

Amazon is seeing greater tariff expenses like other merchants due to President Donald Trump’s international trade policy. The Iran war is pushing up oil and fuel prices, which could potentially eat into the company’s e-commerce revenue through higher transportation expenses.

Meanwhile, Amazon has been cutting delivery times for orders using a mix of robotics, artificial intelligence (AI) and more efficient warehouses. Faster delivery helped Amazon knock Walmart off of the No. 1 spot as the nation’s largest firm by sales in February, according to Fortune, which ranks the top 500 U.S. companies by total revenue for their fiscal years.

In May, Amazon said it was quickly creating tiny order processing hubs in dozens of U.S. and overseas locations for 30 minute delivery to those who can’t or don’t want to wait to get cough medication to ease flu symptoms or tomatoes for tonight’s supper salad.

The firm said Thursday it more than doubled the number of new users for its online pharmacy service in the first half of the year, and same-day prescription delivery were nearly five times higher.

This year Amazon also shifted its main four-day discount event, Prime Day, from July to June.

Amazon announced a net income of $62.65 billion, or $5.75 a share, for the three months ending June 30. That compared with $18.16 billion or $1.68 a share, in the same quarter last year.

Net sales increased to $200.6 billion from $167.7 billion a year earlier.

Analysts had forecast $197.03 billion in revenue in the latest quarter.

Amazon estimates net sales to reach between $197 billion and $202 billion. Analysts at FactSet predict $203.9 billion

Why this matters

A handful of technology companies are shaping the pace and geography of AI infrastructure. Their spending affects power demand, chip supply, cloud pricing and expectations across public markets.

What to consider next

The key test is whether AWS growth and operating income rise fast enough to justify the larger asset base over several quarters.
