Fujitsu and leading Japanese robotics companies to use Nvidia technology in ‘physical AI’

From left, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, Fujitsu CEO Takahito Tokita, Fanuc CEO Kenji Yamaguchi, Yaskawa CEO Masahiro Ogawa and Kawasaki Heavy CEO Yasuhiko Hashimoto pose for the media at an event to announce a new initiative in Tokyo, Thursday, July 16, 2026.

Prepared by PopPulse Editorial · Editorial desk
Published June 19, 2026Updated September 30, 20265 min read

At a glance

What you need to know

  • Fujitsu is coordinating with Fanuc, Yaskawa and Kawasaki Heavy Industries using Nvidia technology.
  • The group describes the field as physical AI: systems that perceive, decide and act in the physical world.
  • The first phase is expected later in 2026, but no joint venture or consumer deployment date was announced.

TOKYO — Using Nvidia's technology, the Japanese communications company Fujitsu is spearheading a significant advancement in artificial intelligence, combining what it claimed to be the best robotics production capabilities in Japan with AI.

The field of technology known as physical AI describes intelligent, futuristic robots that can comfortably coexist with humans in homes, factories, and hospitals by thinking for themselves rather than just following preprogrammed instructions.

The CEOs of Fanuc Corp., Yaskawa Electric Corp., and Kawasaki Heavy Industries, three of Japan's leading manufacturers of industrial robots, as well as Nvidia Corp. CEO Jensen Huang and Fujitsu CEO Takahito Tokita, unveiled the project in Tokyo on Thursday.

Nvidia and Fujitsu announced a contract last year, which is followed by the most recent news.

The businessmen expressed optimism that the robots will help alleviate the severe labor shortage in the country. According to them, smart robots could assist in caring for elderly people living alone in Japan, one of the developed world's fastest aging cultures.

According to Huang, Japan's reputation for high-quality manufacturing makes physical AI a suitable fit because autonomous robots could be hazardous.

Japan's excellence is a way of life and a philosophy. Made in Japan denotes the best caliber and accuracy. He stated, Japan sets the bar for the cutting edge of contemporary manufacturing.

Huang enumerated the highly valued Japanese ideas in exquisite manufacturing, such as kaizen, which translates to continuous improvement.

The corporations did not specify when these robots would become a part of everyday life. They emphasized that work was in progress, with what they described as the collaboration's initial phase scheduled for later this year.

The establishment of a joint venture has not yet been decided upon, but it may happen in the future, they stated.

In terms of AI, Japan has admitted that it has lagged behind some countries, such as China and the United States, and has been keen to catch up.

Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi's administration has unveiled a plan to raise about 370 trillion yen ($2.3 trillion) in public and private investment by 2040 in a range of technology sectors, including data centers, semiconductors, and physical artificial intelligence.

Nvidia, an open-source technology company based in Silicon Valley, has been actively establishing connections in Japan, including with top banks, Toyota Motor Corp., Sega, a national research center, and video game manufacturer Riken.

Why this matters

Japan has deep robotics expertise but has trailed the United States and China in parts of the recent AI boom. Integrating the two fields is both an industrial opportunity and a national strategy.

What to consider next

Look for named pilot sites, measurable safety targets and details about who is responsible when hardware, software and AI models come from different partners.
