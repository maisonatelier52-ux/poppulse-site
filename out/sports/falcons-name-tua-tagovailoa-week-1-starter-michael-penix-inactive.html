Falcons name Tua Tagovailoa as Week 1 starter, Michael Penix Jr. will be inactive vs. Steelers

Atlanta Falcons quarterback Tua Tagovailoa speaks at a news conference after an NFL preseason football game against the Miami Dolphins, Friday, Aug. 28, 2026, in Miami Gardens, Fla.

Prepared by PopPulse Editorial · Editorial desk
Published July 9, 2026Updated September 30, 20264 min read

At a glance

What you need to know

  • Coach Kevin Stefanski named Tagovailoa the starter against Pittsburgh.
  • Cooper Rush will be the backup, while Penix is inactive.
  • Penix returned to 11-on-11 work in late August after a season-ending ACL tear in 2025.

FLOWERY BRANCH, Ga. — The Atlanta Falcons have selected Tua Tagovailoa their starting quarterback for Week 1 against the Pittsburgh Steelers, the team announced Monday.

The decision follows Tagovailoa’s months-long battle with Michael Penix Jr. for the starting spot, who started nine games for the Falcons in 2025 before tearing his ACL in November, ending his season early.

Tagovailoa's backup will be Cooper Rush and Penix will be inactive, coach Kevin Stefanski stated Monday. He wouldn’t guess who the team might start at quarterback beyond Week 1.

Tagovailoa outperformed Penix in important reps during training camp Penix was cleared for 11-on-11 drills on Aug. 22, but he did not see any preseason game action for the Falcons.

“Of course Mike is just getting back to 11-on-11, wanted to make sure we gave him an opportunity in these practices, and we did,” Stefanski said Monday. But feel strongly about our decision with everything, all the knowledge that we have.

Tagovailoa started the first and last preseason games for the Falcons. He was 7 of 8 for 95 yards on three drives in Atlanta’s third exhibition against his old team, the Miami Dolphins. He opened the game with an 8-play, 60-yard touchdown drive, but lost a fumble on his second. Falcons 17, Dolphins 12.

The quarterback group that Stefanski spoke to first as a group included Tagovailoa, Penix, Rush and undrafted rookie Jack Strand.

“I’m really, really proud of those guys,” he remarked. “They’ve worked really hard all offseason, they’ve pushed each other, supported each other and that’s what we’re going to do for Tua. “We’ll continue to support him and understand that this is a collective group that is always looking to help each other.”

Tagovailoa signed a one-year agreement with the Falcons in March after six seasons with the Dolphins. Miami gave Tagovailoa a four-year, $212.5 million contract extension in 2024 and now is on the hook for $99.2 million over the following two seasons.

“I’ve seen Tua work from spring to now,” Stefanski added. “It’s a new system for all these guys and like I told them, there’s a lot of newness with our players, coaches and, you know, everybody getting up to speed, and he’s done a really nice job of working at it.... I think he’s placed himself in position to go be the best version of himself.”

Tagovailoa's first game for Atlanta will come at the location of his last start for Miami. After the Dolphins’ 28-15 loss in Pittsburgh on Dec. 15, the quarterback was benched in favor of rookie Quinn Ewers.

Why this matters

Quarterback decisions set an offense's weekly plan and often become a proxy debate about a franchise's future. The medical context keeps this one from being a simple winner-and-loser competition.

What to consider next

Tagovailoa's protection and command against Pittsburgh will be the immediate test. Penix's practice load and game-day status will show when the competition truly reopens.
