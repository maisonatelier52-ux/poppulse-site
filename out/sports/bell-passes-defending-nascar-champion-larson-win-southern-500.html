Bell passes defending NASCAR champion Larson to win the Southern 500 to start the Chase

Christopher Bell celebrates in Victory Lane after winning a NASCAR Cup Series auto race Sunday, Sept. 6, 2026, in Darlington, S.C.

Prepared by PopPulse Editorial · Editorial desk
Published July 13, 2026Updated September 30, 20265 min read

At a glance

What you need to know

  • Bell took the lead from Kyle Larson with 18 laps to go.
  • The win ended a 33-race drought after seven runner-up finishes this season.
  • The Southern 500 opened NASCAR's Chase at Darlington Raceway.

DARLINGTON, S.C. — This season Christopher Bell is now second to none.

Bell’s maddening season of seven runner-up finishes without a win finally ended with a victory at the Southern 500 on Sunday nite.

The Joe Gibbs Racing driver passed defending NASCAR champion Kyle Larson with 18 laps remaining at Darlington Raceway and held on for the victory to open the Chase and snap a 33-race winless streak that dated to Bristol last September.

“Darlington is the epitome of NASCAR,” added Bell. “That’s better than a dream come true.

It was his first in 15 career Cup races at Darlington and moved him into second in the Chase standings behind teammate Denny Hamlin.

No weeks off. “Right back to work at Gateway next week,” he continued. “I still think I’m going to have to win more races if I want to win the championship.

This season, Gibbs finished second, and five-time winner Tyler Reddick was third. Nine of the top 10 were drivers for Chase. Ryan Blaney placed fourth, followed by Larson, Chase Briscoe, Josh Berry, Bubba Wallace, Joey Logano and Ryan Preece.

In that group, Berry was the only driver not driving for Chase.

As the runner-up crossed the finish line — and the driver’s frustration — mounted, crew chief Adam Stevens sought to keep Bell focused. It was vital for him to know that the reason he finished second was not because his team wasn’t doing everything it could to achieve. “You have to really come into each weekend with a clean slate and he’s done a fantastic job with that,” Stevens said.

Larson took a chance during the sixth and final yellow, remaining out when other drivers like Bell pitted for new tires.

And for a time, Larson’s gamble looked like it would be the right call as he built a big lead on the restart and forced Bell and the field to come to him. Bell did, finally, laying low on the track, putting Larson in his rear view mirror.

On the last yellow, Larson assumed more vehicles would have waited out of the pits to offer him some cover and distance before the fresh-tire drivers got rolling. But Bell got to second so quick, I was right into defense,” Larson said. “I couldn’t run the lines so I ended up taking care of my tires.

Hamlin entered the race with a 25-point lead over second-seeded Ryan Blaney. The regulations for NASCAR’s postseason changed this year, eliminating the eliminations. All 16 drivers competed in all 10 playoff races.

He's still out front, 12 points ahead of teammate Bell.

Other Chaser problems Problems were not limited to Hamlin among the 16 Chase contenders. Hendrick Motorsports William Byron was on his way to a top 10 finish when he spun out what he thought was a flat tire. Smoke was pouring out of Byron's Chevrolet after a trip into the pits and he headed back to the garage to call it a day.

Roush Fenway Keselowski Racing had high hopes with two of its three drivers in the Chase, Chris Buescher and Ryan Preece. But Buescher could not get going at Darlington, and was running at the rear of the pack for most of the run.

3 in a row denied Briscoe was trying to make some history with his third consecutive Southern 500 — only Hall of Famer Jeff Gordon had ever won more with four in a row from 1995-98 — and he battled off a pit-road penalty for running out of fuel at the end of stage two that dropped him from the lead to 20th.

But in the end he could not match Bell.

Hamlin’s problems Hamlin, the top seed in NASCAR's Chase, was running in the top five until Michael McDowell hit him from behind on lap 237 and his vehicle smacked the wall in turn four.

Right away Hamlin told his team, “The toe links are gone. But the incident didn’t bring out a caution and Hamlin had to keep racing, sliding to 20th as he and his pit crew was still leading by 12 points over teammate Bell.crew waited for a chance to repair the damaged Joe Gibbs Racing Toyota.

The toe links remained intact, but there was a lot of damage and Hamlin was a lap down. Hamlin wound up 18th.

“For sure it wasn’t the finish we were hoping for,” Hamlin said. “You know, we got on it all day.

Preece, who punched his ticket to the postseason by winning at Daytona last week, promised to keep the aggressiveness because as the 16th seed his team had nothing to lose. That tactic paid off as he was in the top 10 most of the first half of the race. But he couldn't sustain it and finished 10th.

And with 23XI co-owner Michael Jordan watching from the pits, his two Chase drivers in Reddick and Wallace fell from 1-2 at the end of the opening stage to out of the mix down the stretch.

Why this matters

Playoff formats reward timing as much as season-long strength. Bell's win arrived at the moment when a single result has the greatest strategic value.

What to consider next

Watch whether the Darlington setup translates to other high-wear tracks and whether the pit crew can match the late-race execution that secured this win.
