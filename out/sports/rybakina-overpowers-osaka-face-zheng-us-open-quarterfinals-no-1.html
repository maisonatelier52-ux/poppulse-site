Rybakina overpowers Osaka, will face Zheng in US Open quarterfinals with a chance to become No. 1

Elena Rybakina, of Kazakhstan, left, greets Naomi Osaka, of Japan, after winning a match during the fourth round of the U.S. Open tennis championships, Monday, Sept. 7, 2026, in New York.

At a glance

What you need to know

  • Elena Rybakina defeated Naomi Osaka 6–1, 6–4 in 66 minutes.
  • A semifinal place would move Rybakina to No. 1 in the WTA rankings.
  • Zheng Qinwen reached the quarterfinals after rallying from 5–0 down in consecutive matches.

NEW YORK — Elena Rybakina crushed Naomi Osaka at the U.S. Open and is one win away from the No. 1 ranking in women's tennis.

All she needs to do is beat Zheng Qinwen, which won't be easy. Even when Zheng falls, and falls hard, she will not stay down.

Power-hitting Grand Slam champions Rybakina and Osaka met on Monday with Rybakina winning 6-1, 6-4 to reach the last eight. If Rybakina reaches the semifinals at Flushing Meadows, she will be guarantyd to replace Aryna Sabalenka at No. 2 as the top seed in the WTA rankings.

“Of course I want it, I don’t know anyone who wouldn’t wish this, but undoubtedly that’s one of my ambitions, Rybakina added. I try to do everything I can, battle till the finish my next match and let's see what's going to happen. I try to approach the same manner.

Zheng will try to halt her. She produced a gutsy escape from 5-0 down for the second straight match. The 2024 Olympic gold winner from China defeated No. 8 seed Iga Swiatek 7-5, 6-3 to become the first qualifier to reach the final eight in Flushing Meadows since Emma Raducanu won the title in 2021.

Zheng fell behind Swiatek, the 2022 U.S. Open champion, 5-0 in the opening set after rallying from a 5-0 deficit in the third set to beat No. 22 Madison Keys 1-6, 7-6 (3), 7-5 two days earlier.

“I guess it’s not lucky to come back 0-5 yesterday, that’s all I can say,” Zheng remarked.

Rybakina captured the Australian Open this year for her second major win, and her No. 2 ranking is a career high. Even if she drops out of the top spot on Wednesday, she can still reach No. 1 if Sabalenka, Coco Gauff or Jessica Pegula don’t win the title.

Osaka, who has won two of her four Grand Slam championships at the U.S. Open, appeared to be hampered by a foot or ankle ailment but Rybakina was just too good. Rybakina finished it off with an ace and the match lasted one hour and six minutes. She leads the WTA Tour in that department.

Resurgent Zheng goes from qualies to quarters Zheng was a quarterfinalist at the U.S. Open in 2023 and 2024 – the year she made it to the Australian Open final – but she had to go through qualification this year to get into the main event after her ranking fell following elbow surgery. Raducanu's success five years ago was the first time a qualifier has won a Grand Slam title in the Open era, which started in 1968.

Swiatek failed to convert two set points when serving at 5-1 and Zheng fought off another when Swiatek served with a 5-3 advantage. She said she was so concentrated on each point, she even forgot the score.

Swiatek entered with a 7-1 record versus Zheng, her sole setback coming two years ago in the Olympic quarterfinals in Paris. Zheng fought back from 4-0 down in the second set that day to end Swiatek’s 25-match winning run at Roland Garros, where she had won four French Open titles.

Swiatek had treatment on her left thigh in the second set but maintained she was not suffering any physical or emotional concerns.

“It was more that I started making mistakes from easy balls and Qinwen stopped doing mistakes,” Swiatek remarked. “She played the balls better.
