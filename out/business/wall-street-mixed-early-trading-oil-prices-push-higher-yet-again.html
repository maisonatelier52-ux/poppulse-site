Wall Street mixed in early trading while oil prices push higher yet again

Specialist Michael Pistillo works on the floor of the New York Stock Exchange, Thursday, Aug. 27, 2026, in New York.

Prepared by PopPulse Editorial · Editorial desk
Published June 10, 2026Updated September 30, 20266 min read

At a glance

What you need to know

  • Dow futures fell more sharply than Nasdaq futures in the early September 8 session.
  • Higher oil can help producers while hurting transport, consumer and rate-sensitive businesses.
  • Early trading and futures do not establish the day's final direction.

US. stock futures were neutral early Tuesday as a flare-up in the widening Middle East war sent oil prices higher.

S&P 500 futures slipped 0.3% ahead of the bell, while Dow Jones Industrial Average futures lost 0.8%. Futures on the Nasdaq were flat.

The renewed war in Yemen between a major U.S. ally and Iranian-backed rebels has escalated with a series of Houthi strikes against oil facilities and utilities in Saudi Arabia’s southern area.

Brent crude, the global benchmark, rose 1.5% to $98.48 a barrel, its best since almost six weeks, as tensions simmered in the six-month U.S. battle with Iran.

Benchmark U.S. crude jumped 2.5% to $93.79 a barrel, or 40% higher than it was in late February just before the U.S. and Israel began their attacks on Iran.

Higher oil prices are pinching consumers and companies, making this week’s inflation numbers from the government even more important than usual. The Federal Reserve meets next week to decide whether to reduce, hike or leave interest rates unchanged. The decision has been compounded by a rather solid job market and historically low unemployment.

On Thursday, the U.S. government will release its August data on inflation at the wholesale level, known as the Producer Price Index, or PPI. It provides price data for companies before they pass the prices on to customers.

The more closely regarded Consumer Price Index is out Friday. The CPI gives data on the change in prices for some grocery goods, furniture and clothes among others.

Tokyo’s Nikkei 225 gave up early gains to fall 1.7% to 65,269.33 in Asia as big exporters were sold on a jump in the value of the Japanese yen.

Shares of Toyota Motor Corp sank 4.1% while electronics maker Panasonic Holdings Corp fell 5.8%.

The U.S. dollar slipped to 154.25 Japanese yen early Tuesday from 154.34 yen. The yen has rallied against the dollar in recent days on speculation that the U.S. and Japanese governments could intervene to stop the currency from falling further. Last week, the dollar briefly shot up to nearly 160 yen.

The government said the economy grew a little quicker in the April-June quarter than previously estimated, at an annual rate of 1.4%. The annualized growth rate had earlier been put at 1.1% by the Cabinet Office.

The revision shows better company investment than previously reported, but overall investment still declined 0.9%, said Norihiro Yamaguchi of Oxford Economics in a commentary.

“The boost to consumption from April-May policy measures is already fading and supply side driven inflation will accelerate ahead as firms will pass on increased costs, deteriorating consumers’ purchasing power,” he said.

South Korea’s Kospi dropped 0.6% to 6,954.52 after an early rise. Samsung Electronics shares erased early gains to fall 0.2%.

Hong Kong’s Hang Seng fell 0.4% to 25,317.18 and the Shanghai Composite index moved up 0.2% to 3,940.55.

China reported its exports surged 25 percent year-on-year in August on robust demand for vehicles and high tech items.

Why this matters

Market shorthand can make normal cross-currents sound contradictory. Understanding index construction helps readers avoid turning a brief move into a broad economic conclusion.

What to consider next

Check closing levels, bond yields and oil futures together. If the explanation is right, the relationships should be visible beyond a few minutes of premarket trading.
