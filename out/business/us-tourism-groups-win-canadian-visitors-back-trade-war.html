US tourism groups want to win Canadian visitors back. A testy trade war isn’t helping

City Councillor Renaldo Agostino looks at the Detroit skyline across the Detroit River Monday, Aug. 24, 2026, in Windsor, Ontario

Prepared by PopPulse Editorial · Editorial desk
Published July 24, 2026Updated September 30, 20264 min read

At a glance

What you need to know

  • Canadian return border crossings fell 25% in 2025, Statistics Canada data cited by AP showed.
  • A weaker Canadian dollar and higher travel costs compounded the political backlash.
  • Warm-weather U.S. destinations are watching the winter season as the next major test.

LAS VEGAS — The Trump administration on Sunday asked the Supreme Court to allow new limitations on mail-in ballots to stand before the midterms, after they were halted by a federal judge.

Over the past year, Josh Loewen couldn’t help but notice the appeals from American tourism organizations were everyplace. Billboards and banners screamed love for Canada from state and city. Online and social media marketing promoted special deals for passengers from north of the border.

Vancouver resident and marketing executive Loewen said he understood the conciliatory overtures. President Donald Trump returned to office and made comments about declaring Canada the 51st state. The number of Canadians who headed south to the United States plummeted. An bitter trade conflict between the two countries now threatens to deepen the informal boycott just as it looked to be lessening significantly.

“It's a pretty big ask right now,” said Loewen, 45, of the U.S. tourism industry's attempts to lure Canadians like him back before a new president takes the White House. It's just a waste of time.

Relations between the U.S. and Canadian administrations have plummeted dramatically since their trade talks fell up last month. Trump placed import tariffs of up to 50% on several Canadian items. The Canadian government retaliated in kind. Trump has instructed the U.S. administration to change the name of the border-crossing Lake Ontario to “Lake America.” Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney called remarks from Trump’s Cabinet members “childish and undignified.”

The split between the two countries hasn't stopped state and municipal tourist officials, and iconic attractions such as Graceland, from trying to entice Canadians back.

This summer, New York State launched a NY Loves Canada campaign which featured discounts at hotels, restaurants and attractions. Some downtown Las Vegas hotels are valuing the Canadian dollar the same as the U.S. dollar to give Canadians more bang for their buck. Last month Las Vegas tourism officials were in Canada meeting with travel advisors, tour operators and airline reps.

“We’re here to let you know that we care about Canada,” Steve Hill, head of the Las Vegas Convention and Visitors Authority, said during a visit to Vancouver.

The outreach is also going on a nationwide scale. Brand USA, the marketing agency for the U.S. tourism sector, will expand and rebrand an earlier program called Canada Connect, bringing its Travel Week trade-event series to Canada for the first time in October.

Travel to U.S. from Canada still considerably below 2024 levels Canada has been the biggest sender of overnight international tourists to the United States in the past. Both same-day visits and lengthier stays plummeted in last year's consumer-led backlash. Return border crossings by Canadian citizens fell by 25% in 2025 compared with the previous year, and they spent around $2.4 billion (CA$3.3 billion) less on trips to the U.S., according to Statistics Canada, the country’s main statistical agency.

The retreat corresponded with other obstacles, including a lower Canadian dollar, making travel to the U.S. more expensive, along with higher rates for airfares and hotels. Statistics Canada data suggest Canadian aviation traffic to the U.S. started to decline in September 2023.

But the steep drop in travel in the U.S. that accompanied Trump’s second administration and persisted into this year was an indication of “a persistent shift away from the United States by Canadian residents in their travel preferences,” agency analysts said in a July study.

Border crossings increased modestly in May, June and July, the agency said, offering tentative signs of progress. The past two months overlapped with the co-hosted 2026 World Cup in the U.S., Canada and Mexico. In the early weeks of the competition, Canada’s national soccer team was in action, giving some Canadians a cause to fly south. Carney was at the last game in New Jersey with Trump.

Most of the increase in cross-border traffic was made up of automobile trips, with air travel to the U.S. declining from year-earlier levels every month through June, Statistics Canada said. The U.S. National Travel and Tourism Office expects that Canadians made even fewer overnight visits, which usually mean more tourism spending, in the first six months of 2026 than in the same time last year.

Snowbirds could be a barometer of Canadian sentiment Trump declared duties on $20 billion worth of Canadian goods would take effect in 30 days, one day after the World Cup wrapped. The duties were meant to address U.S. concerns over Canadian obstacles to American-made autos, alcohol and dairy, the White House said.

“You went from this really high, exciting moment for the U.S. in terms of international attention, to the next week, it’s negative again,” said Deborah Friedland, a hospitality consultant at financial services firm Eisner Advisory Group. “It’s like two steps backward, one step forward.”

As summer fades, several of the U.S. attractions most reliant on Canadian visitors are entering their most crucial season. The next months will be a test of whether the grassroots boycott will carry through another winter when huge numbers of Canadian snowbirds generally travel to warm-weather haunts in Florida, Arizona and California when temperatures dip at home.

“I’d be surprised if we’re talking about a year from now and suddenly you see this massive uptick in Canadian travel over the winter months,” Friedland said.

Not all in the tourism business are alarmed. Jennifer Adams, tourist director for the Destin-Fort Walton Beach area in Florida’s Panhandle, said she didn’t worry when reports about Canadians avoiding vacation to the U.S. initially appeared last year.

“I believe our message is strong,” she said, adding that, “the thing for us is to let the Canadian family know that they are welcomed here and we are committed to giving them a great experience when they get here.”

The state marketing organization Visit Florida says Florida had a 7% drop in Canadian visitors in 2025 compared with the previous year. Sister group Visit California, using data from worldwide research and consulting company Tourism Economics, estimated a 20% drop in the number of Canadians visiting the Golden State.

Loewen, the Vancouver marketing professional, said his family liked to visit the United States, but for some Canadians a promise of a wonderful vacation isn’t enough. He and his wife and their two children often went to San Diego, Portland and Seattle. This year they went to Mexico instead.

The hesitance, Loewen added, has little to do with the U.S. being able to provide a wonderful vacation. The question is whether Canadians want to put their money there, given the high level of hostility between their country and the U.S. Loewen said his family hasn't been to the U.S. since Trump's inauguration and won't go until there is a new president.

Eileen March, a Calgary life coach, said she made a similar choice. Trump’s vows to make Canada the 51st state, his trade policies — and the broader sense of no longer feeling safe or welcome in the United States — were all part of it, March said.

“As time has gone on I’ve started to have second thoughts,” she remarked. The most recent wave of tariffs “just cemented my decision of not traveling in the U.S. at all while he is in office.” She won't even schedule flights with U.S. layovers. March, 41, realizes four years is a long time to be away. She’s willing to go even longer.

“I think that will really depend on who is elected next, their values and the relationship they try to foster or repair with Canada,” she added.

Why this matters

Canadian visitors support hotels, restaurants and seasonal jobs across border states and warm-weather destinations. A political dispute can therefore become a local-business shock far from national capitals.

What to consider next

Winter travel by Canadian snowbirds will provide the clearest near-term test. Compare overnight stays and spending, not just total crossings.
