Asian shares and US futures decline, while oil prices press higher

Currency traders work near a screen showing the Korea Composite Stock Price Index (KOSPI), the foreign exchange rate between U.S. dollar and South Korean won, SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics Co. stock price at the foreign exchange dealing room of the Hana Bank headquarters in Seoul, South Korea, Tuesday, Sept. 8, 2026.

Prepared by PopPulse Editorial · Editorial desk
Published June 13, 2026Updated September 30, 20265 min read

At a glance

What you need to know

  • Asian shares and U.S. futures fell as oil prices rose for a third session.
  • Middle East attacks increased concern about supply disruption and inflation.
  • Investors were also waiting for U.S. producer- and consumer-price reports.

BANGKOK — Asian equities and U.S. futures tumbled Tuesday as renewed unrest in the Middle East sent oil prices higher.

The renewed battle between a key U.S. ally and Iranian-backed rebels in Yemen has been thrown fuel on by a new round of Houthi attacks on oil installations and utilities in Saudi Arabia’s southern area.

Tokyo’s Nikkei 225 turned around early gains to fall 1.7% to 65,269.33, with big exporters sold down on a spike in the value of the Japanese yen.

Toyota Motor Corp. shares slid 4.1%. Electronics producer Panasonic Holdings Corp. dropped 5.8%.

The U.S. dollar fell to 153.73 Japanese yen early Tuesday from 154.34 yen. The yen has strengthened against the dollar in recent days on hopes that the U.S. and Japanese governments may intervene to prevent the yen from declining further. The dollar momentarily soared to around 160 yen last week.

The government said the economy grew at a slightly quicker annual rate of 1.4% in the April-June quarter than previously reported. The annualized growth rate had earlier been put at 1.1% by the Cabinet Office.

The revision shows better business investment than previously reported, but overall investment still declined, at minus 0.9%, said Norihiro Yamaguchi of Oxford Economics in a statement.

He said: “The boost to consumption from policy measures in April-May is already fading and inflation driven by supply-side factors will pick up speed as firms pass on higher costs, hurting consumers’ purchasing power.

South Korea’s Kospi gave up early gains to fall 0.6% to 6,954.52 Samsung Electronics shares erased earlier gains to drop 0.2%.

Hong Kong’s Hang Seng shed 0.3% to 25,345.04 and the Shanghai Composite index added 0.2% to 3,941.44.

China reported exports in August surged 25 percent year on year, helped by robust demand for vehicles and high-tech goods.

The S&P/ASX 200 in Australia dropped 1% to 8,920.80.

The S&P 500 sank 0.4% Friday, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average dipped 0.5%. The Nasdaq composite dropped 0.3%.

This week Wall Street will get key reports on inflation. On Thursday, the U.S. will release its data on inflation at the wholesale level, termed the Producer Price Index, or PPI, for August. It gives firms advance warning about prices, which they then pass on to customers.

The U.S. will release its more widely recognized Consumer Price Index, for August, on Friday. The CPI is extensively followed and includes data of price fluctuations for certain supermarket products, furniture, apparel and other categories. It also breaks down price fluctuations for everything from car maintenance to travel and eating in restaurants.

Brent crude, the worldwide benchmark, rose 1.4% to $98.40 a barrel in energy markets early Tuesday as tensions simmered in the six-month U.S. battle with Iran.

U.S. crude benchmark gained 2.6% to $93.88 a barrel.

Elsewhere in the currency markets the euro was flat at $1.1624.

Why this matters

Energy prices transmit geopolitical risk into household inflation, company margins and interest-rate expectations. That pathway is more durable than any one day's index move.

What to consider next

Compare the subsequent inflation releases, closing prices and physical oil-supply data before concluding that the session marked a lasting trend.
