‘Widow’s Bay’ dominates with 8 Emmys at early ceremony and ‘The Pitt’ takes 4

Ernest Harden Jr. poses in the press room with the award for outstanding guest actor in a drama series for “The Pitt” during night two of the Creative Arts Emmy Awards on Sunday, Sept. 6, 2026, in Los Angeles.

Prepared by PopPulse Editorial · Editorial desk
Published July 20, 2026Updated September 30, 20264 min read

At a glance

What you need to know

  • Widow's Bay won eight awards across cinematography, sound, music, production design and acting.
  • The Pitt won four, including guest actor for Ernest Harden Jr.
  • Creative Arts results recognize the crafts that shape a series before the televised main ceremony.

LOS ANGELES — “Widow’s Bay” nabbed eight early Emmys for its first season Sunday at a presentation eight days before the main awards show and aired, and “The Pitt” had a solid second-year showing with four of its own. Rob Reiner posthumously earned an acting Emmy nearly nine months after his killing.

“DTF St. Louis” received six prizes for limited series, including co-stars and experienced performers David Harbor and Linda Cardellini, both of whom won their first Emmys.

Apple TV Plus’s horror comedy “Widow’s Bay,” in which Matthew Rhys plays the mayor of a small New England town that is haunted by a centuries-old curse, won categories including cinematography, sound, music and production design.

Betty Gilpin took home best guest actress in a comedy for her work on the show, and she gave a rapid-fire funny poetry as her acceptance speech. It was “our business is stupid but actors are the bomb” and “let’s all lean more toward Sam Shepard and less toward Sam Altman.”

‘The Pitt’ scores strong sophomore showing HBO Max’s day-in-the-life medical series “The Pitt” — the year’s best-nominated series with 25 nominations and the reigning best drama winner for its first season — won Sunday in categories including prosthetic makeup and best guest actor in a drama for 73-year-old first-time nominee Ernest Harden Jr.

Harden, who won for her portrayal of the last days of Louie Cloverfield, the emergency room’s ever-present and always battling alcoholic on “The Pitt,” wept when her name was mentioned.

“I’m just on cloud 25,” he remarked backstage at the Peacock Theater in Los Angeles. “I wouldn’t say it’s a total shock, because ‘The Pitt’ is such a powerful show to come from, but to have it in your hands is unreal.”

Harden’s first credited speaking role was in the 1975 Robert Redford-Faye Dunaway drama Three Days of the Condor and he had a three-season stint on the comedy The Jeffersons in the 1970s, but has worked for decades without awards acclaim.

Singer-songwriter Andrew Bird and Gavin Brivik won for original music and lyrics for a song they penned about the character.

“It’s not a music-centric show,” Bird stated backstage. But they offered us a great moment with a character we love.

Rebranding the Creative Arts Emmys and adding some star power The Television Academy hands out well more than 100 primetime Emmys, and most of them will be handed out this weekend in a pair of events. Once known as the Creative Arts Emmys, they’re now being relaunched as just the first two nites of the 78th Emmy Awards.

Most of the weekend’s prizes are for behind-the-scenes crew members, but they also feature the guest acting awards, which often go to greater celebrities. And the star power of the shows got a boost when five categories usually on the main telecast were bumped to the earlier weekend, including best supporting actor in a limited series, won by Harbor in his third nomination, and best supporting actress in a limited series, won by Linda Cardellini in her fourth nomination in an elite TV career that included roles on “Freaks and Geeks” and “Mad Men.”

“Thank you to everybody that helped me along the way through the years to get to this moment of a real dream come true,” Cardellini said from the platform, through tears.

Rob Reiner gets a muted ovation Otherwise moving at a brisk speed and carefree in tone, there was a long, thoughtful applause when Reiner was revealed as best guest actor in a comedy for his role on “The Bear.” Among those in the throng paying tribute to him was Jamie Lee Curtis, a friend and fellow guest star on “Bear” with Reiner.

Reiner and his wife, Michele Singer Reiner, were killed in December and their son, who has pled not guilty, has been charged with stabbing them to death. His win is approximately 50 years after he won two Emmy Awards for his acting on “All in the Family.”

The ceremonies Saturday and Sunday will be edited together for broadcast Sept. 12 on FXX and streaming on Hulu the next day. The primetime Sept. 14 ceremony will be held at the Peacock Theater and presented by Mariska Hargitay and will air live on NBC and stream live on Peacock.

“Widow’s Bay” and “The Pitt” might add as many as six more Emmys on that last nite.

Another Apple TV Emmy newcomer from “Breaking Bad” creator Vince Gilligan, “Pluribus,” which could challenge “The Pitt” in the top drama categories, received four medals Sunday nite including editing, production design and sound mixing.

‘Spider-Noir’ and Shailene Woodley Are Also Winners Shailene Woodley won best guest actress in a drama for her work on Paradise .It was her first Emmy award.

Amazon Prime Video dropped the Nicolas Cage sitcom “Spider-Noir” last week after one season, but it did win five Emmys including for visual effects and stunts.

Hargitay was a big winner this weekend, taking two Emmys for the documentary she created about her mother, Jayne Mansfield, My Mom Jayne as well as the best actress in a drama Emmy she received in 2006 for Law & Order Special Victims Unit.

Bad Bunny also won his first career Emmy for his Super Bowl halftime show, taking seven trophies during the opening of the weekend’s presentations on Saturday.

Why this matters

Craft awards make visible the collaborative work—sound, editing, design, makeup and cinematography—that determines how television feels long before an actor delivers a line.

What to consider next

Compare the early results with the main ceremony, while avoiding the assumption that strength in craft categories mechanically predicts best-series winners.
